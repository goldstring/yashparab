Overview

Northbeam Analytics needed to replace a batch fraud-scoring job that ran every six hours with a system that could catch fraudulent transactions before they settled. The existing setup meant real losses were confirmed a full day after the fact, and the rules-based scoring logic hadn't been retrained in over a year. My team was asked to design and ship a real-time replacement without disrupting checkout latency for legitimate customers.

The problem

Fraud patterns shift week to week, so a static rules engine was steadily losing ground to new attack patterns while also generating too many false positives on legitimate high-value purchases. Any replacement needed to score transactions in well under 100ms to stay inside the checkout flow, retrain on a tight schedule without a lengthy deployment process, and give the risk team visibility into why a transaction was flagged.

  • Existing batch scoring introduced up to 6 hours of detection lag.
  • False-positive rate on legitimate transactions was above acceptable thresholds.
  • No automated way to detect when the model's input distribution had drifted.

Approach & architecture

We split the system into a streaming feature pipeline and a two-stage scoring layer. Transaction events flow through Kafka into a Spark Structured Streaming job that computes rolling behavioral features (velocity, device reputation, geo-consistency) and writes them to a low-latency Redis feature store. A lightweight gradient-boosted model runs first as a cheap filter; transactions it flags as ambiguous are passed to a PyTorch deep model for a second opinion, and both feed a calibrated ensemble score returned to checkout in under 40ms end to end.

Deployment runs on Kubernetes with canary rollouts: every new model version serves 5% of shadow traffic before promotion, and Grafana dashboards track precision/recall, latency percentiles and feature drift in real time so the risk team can catch regressions before they reach full traffic.

Results

Six months after full rollout, detection lag dropped from hours to milliseconds and the false-positive rate on legitimate transactions fell by more than half, which measurably reduced support tickets from declined purchases.

Challenges & learnings

The hardest part wasn't the model — it was keeping training-time and serving-time features consistent. We lost two weeks early on to a subtle skew between how a feature was computed in batch training versus the streaming pipeline, which taught us to treat the feature store schema as a contract with its own versioning and tests, not an implementation detail.
